A note on these lines

Every measure on this page counts mentions — reports filed, share of coverage, articles read. None of them counts cases, deaths, wind speed or rainfall. Attention rises and falls with the news cycle rather than with the danger, and the worst-hit places are often the least written about. For what actually happened, use the official counts below.

What actually happened — official counts

  • National Hurricane CenterLive forecasts, cones, wind speeds and advisories. The authority on Atlantic storms.
  • HURDAT2Best-track record of every Atlantic storm since 1851, as plain text.
  • NWS alertsLive American watches and warnings, feeding The Wires above.
  • WHO outbreak newsVerified global outbreak reports with confirmed case counts.
  • CDC investigationsAmerican case counts as they are confirmed.
  • GDACSAutomatic severity and population-exposure estimates within hours of an event.
  • ReliefWebUN situation reports: people affected, displaced, reached.
  • EM-DATDeaths and economic losses for 27,000 disasters since 1900. Free for research use.
  • USGS earthquakesMagnitude, depth and shaking intensity, worldwide, in near real time.
  • Our World in DataLong-run mortality and disease series, with sources shown.
